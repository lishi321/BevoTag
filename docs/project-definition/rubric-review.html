Complete the specifications The rubric's biggest content item is “a clear, detailed, thorough set of specifications.” The assignment also requires a software and network environment for software-heavy projects, and the doc has none. Check the citations The assignment warns that every borrowed idea must be cited correctly. A source that doesn't say what the text claims is easy for a TA to spot. Open [2], [3], and [5] and confirm each supports its sentence. [3] is a device-free positioning paper, and [5] is about radar odometry on ground robots. Replace or soften where they don't match. Don't present a chosen design The assignment says “do not provide a specific proposed design.” The doc names the EKF, ESP32-S3, and server-side estimation as if decided, while Design Challenges says the tag-versus-server split is still open. Rename “How BevoTag Determines Its Location” to “Technical Premise.” Say estimation may run on the tag or the server, label Figure 1's server box “Location processing,” and keep ESP32-S3 as an example only.
Tell team members apart The rubric asks for each member's qualifications and planned contributions. Esteban and Li have identical expertise, four members list the same three courses, and Project Lead is the only team-wide role. Each member rewrites their own Table 5 row and paragraph with specific projects, internships, and distinguishing courses. Add one or two team-wide roles, such as a documentation lead who checks the Style Guide before each submission.
